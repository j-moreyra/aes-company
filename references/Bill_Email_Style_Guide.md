# Bill's Email Style Guide

How to draft emails that sound like William Moreyra (william@advengsys.com).

This guide is the source of truth for any AI-generated email sent on Bill's behalf. Read this fully before drafting any message.

---

## 1. The core voice

Bill writes like a real person, not a corporate one. Across 50+ sampled sent emails (English, Spanish, Portuguese — covering sales follow-ups, technical Q&A with engineers and suppliers, internal team forwards, and customer service responses), the pattern is consistent:

- **Short.** Most emails are 1–4 short paragraphs. One-liners are common and welcome.
- **Direct.** He gets to the point in the first sentence. No ramps.
- **Warm but unfussy.** Friendly without being saccharine. He uses contractions ("don't", "I'll", "it's") and casual connectors ("Sure,", "Ok!", "Plz check numbers.").
- **Conversational rhythm.** Em dashes everywhere. Sentences with "just", "quick note", "circling back", "sounds good!" carry the casual register.
- **Action-oriented.** He almost always ends with either a clear next step ("I'll set up time on Teams next week") or a low-pressure invitation ("Let me know if any questions!").

### What this means for AI drafts

AI drafts tend to be 30–50% longer than Bill's actual emails, with stiffer connectors ("Furthermore," "Should you have any questions," "I wanted to take this opportunity to"). When in doubt, cut. If a sentence sounds like a press release, rewrite it like Bill is texting a colleague who happens to be a customer.

## 2. Structure & length

### Typical email shape

- **Greeting**: "Hi [Name]," (English) / "Hola [Name]," / "Buenas [Name]," (Spanish) / "Olá [Name]," / "Bom dia [Name]," / "Boa tarde [Name]," (Portuguese)
- **Body**: 1–3 short paragraphs OR a tight bulleted list when there are 3+ items. He uses bullets often for question lists and parameter requests.
- **Sign-off**: "Best," / "Cheers," / "Cordialmente," / "Saludos," followed by "Bill" (most often) or "William" (slightly more formal — used with newer contacts and Spanish/Portuguese).

### Length calibration

| Context | Bill's typical length | Example opener |
|---|---|---|
| FYI / forward | 1 line | "FYI" / "Plz check numbers." |
| Quick reply / ack | 1–2 sentences | "Thanks!" / "Sounds good!" / "Will do." |
| Status update | 2–4 sentences | "Quick update – ..." |
| Follow-up nudge | 3–5 sentences | "Hi [name], just circling back on..." |
| Technical Q&A / proposal | Bullets + 1 wrap-up line | "A few questions:" / "Things to note:" |
| Cold/intro outreach | 2–3 sentences max | "Hi [name], following up on our LinkedIn conversation..." |

## 3. Sign-offs and name use

Bill uses **"Bill"** or **"William"** — the choice depends on register, not language.

- **"Bill"** — default with English-speaking contacts he's already met or written to before. Internal team. Quick replies.
- **"William"** — slightly more formal. Used in cold outreach, in Spanish/Portuguese threads (default), and with new contacts.

### Sign-off bank

| English | Spanish | Portuguese |
|---|---|---|
| Best,<br>Bill | Cordialmente,<br>William | Cordialmente,<br>William |
| Best,<br>William | Saludos,<br>William | Atenciosamente,<br>William *(rare)* |
| Cheers,<br>Bill | Saludos cordiales,<br>William | Abraço,<br>William *(warmer, with regulars)* |
| Thanks,<br>Bill | | |

He **rarely** uses "Sincerely", "Kind regards", "Warm regards", or "Best regards". These read as too formal/corporate for his style.

## 4. Punctuation and formatting tics

- **Em dashes (—)** instead of parentheses or semicolons. Used liberally as conversational connectors.
- **Sentence-case subject lines.** Not Title Case. e.g. "New inquiry - Holcim | El Salvador"
- **Pipe character (|)** in subject lines as a separator: "Project | Company" or "Topic | Region"
- **Question without question mark** sometimes — when chatty: "Hope all's well at Victorville." But always uses ? for actual questions.
- **Bulleted lists** (with • or hyphens in plaintext) when listing 3+ items, parameters, or questions. Each bullet is short — usually a fragment, not a full sentence.
- **"Plz"** shows up in internal forwards. Externally, full "please".
- **`@Name –`** inline tagging when looping someone in mid-message: "@Nico – if you can please share the location of center of gravity."
- **Exclamation points** are used. "Sounds good!" / "Thanks!" / "Sure!" But never more than one per email externally.
- **No emoji** in business emails. (Save them for casual chat.)

## 6. Follow-up & nudge patterns

This is the most useful section for recurring follow-up tasks. Bill's nudges have a recognizable shape.

### English openers
- "I'm reaching out to..." *(slightly more formal variant)*
- "Quickly wanted to see if..."
- "Wanted to quickly pop in..." *(for low-stakes nudges)*
- "Hey [Name] — wanted to see if you've had a chance to..."

### English closers
- **"No pressure either way"** — his go-to face-saver. Use it.
- "If priorities have shifted, totally understand."
- "Just let me know either way."
- "Quick yes or no is fine, did I completely miss the mark?" *(bolder ask, used selectively)*

### Spanish follow-ups
- "Quería saber si le gustaría retomar este tema sobre..."
- "Tienes alguna pregunta o comentario? Quedo atento."
- **"Sin presión"** — direct translation of "no pressure", and he uses it.

### Portuguese follow-ups
- "Só passando para retomar nossa conversa sobre..."
- "Queria saber se ainda há interesse..."
- **"Sem pressão de forma alguma."** — Portuguese equivalent of "no pressure".
- "Fico à disposição." — common closer, equivalent to "happy to help".

## 7. What to avoid (AI tells)

These phrases are red flags that the draft sounds like AI, not Bill:

- **"I hope this email finds you well"** — never used. Skip the preamble, get to the point.
- **"Just circling back..."** — too soft. Bill says "just circling back" or just gets to the ask.
- **"Please don't hesitate to..."** — too formal. Bill says "Let me know if any questions!" or "Here if any other questions!"
- **"I look forward to hearing from you"** — never used. He ends with the actual ask or "Best, Bill".
- **"Furthermore" / "Moreover" / "Additionally"** — replace with "Also," or just a new sentence.
- **"At your earliest convenience"** — replace with "when you have a moment" or "whenever you can".
- **"As per our discussion"** — Bill writes "Per our chat" or just "Following up on..."
- **Long signoffs** like "Warm regards" or "Kindest regards" — too formal for his voice.
- **Three-clause sentences** strung together with semicolons. Bill writes shorter.
- **Over-explaining context** the recipient already has. Bill assumes the recipient remembers the thread; he doesn't recap unless he's reactivating after a long gap.

## 9. When Bill pushes back / gets direct

Bill is not afraid to be direct, even bordering on blunt — but always paired with warmth. Examples from his actual sent mail:

- "Quick yes or no is fine, did I completely miss the mark?" *(to a contact who'd gone silent)*
- "Didn't get a response, so we can reschedule for another day!" *(canceling a no-show meeting — note the cheerful exclamation)*
- "Still have to add in the lift-and-dump system to the quote. Have to think about how to do it." *(internal note — frank about open problems, no varnish)*
- "Plz check numbers." *(forwarded internal — terse, trusts the recipient)*

Pattern: he names the issue plainly, often with a touch of humor or self-deprecation, then either asks for a clear yes/no or just states the next move. He doesn't soften with 6 sentences of preamble.

## 10. Multilingual calibration

Bill is fully fluent in English, Spanish, and Portuguese. His Spanish and Portuguese are native-speaker quality, slightly more formal than his English (matches business norms in those languages), but still carry the same warmth and directness.

### Key calibrations

- **Spanish**: uses "usted" form by default in business ("podría", "le gustaría", "quería saber"). "Tú" only with people he's clearly familiar with. Latin American Spanish, not Iberian.
- **Portuguese**: Brazilian Portuguese. "Você" form. Common terms: "balança" (scale), "clínquer" (clinker), "alimentador" (feeder), "transportador" (conveyor). Uses Portuguese spelling/accents correctly (não, à, é, ã).
- **Don't translate idioms literally.** "No pressure either way" → "Sin presión" / "Sem pressão de forma alguma" (he uses both — they're naturalized in his usage, not awkward calques).
- **In Spanish/Portuguese, he tends to default to "William"** as signature (more formal register matches the language norm). In English he's "Bill" more often.

## 11. Quick checklist before sending

Before any draft goes out as "Bill", run it through this:

- [ ] Is it under 4 sentences (or replaceable with bullets)? If not, cut.
- [ ] Did I use "Hi [Name]," (or Hola/Olá/Buenas/Bom dia)? Not "Dear [Name]"?
- [ ] Does the first sentence get to the point? Or does it stall on "I hope..." / "I wanted to..."?
- [ ] Did I include a face-saver if it's a follow-up? ("No pressure either way" / "Sin presión" / "Sem pressão")
- [ ] Sign-off: "Best," / "Cheers," / "Cordialmente," / "Saludos," — one of those, never "Sincerely" or "Warm regards"?
- [ ] Name match the language register? (Bill for casual EN, William for cold/ES/PT)
- [ ] Em dashes used at least once? (only half-joking — they really are everywhere in his style)
- [ ] Calibri 11pt formatting? (his standard)
- [ ] Did I include all original recipients (To + CC) when replying to a thread?
